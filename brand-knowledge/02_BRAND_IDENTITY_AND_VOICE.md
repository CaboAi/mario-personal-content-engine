# Brand identity and voice

## Positioning line

Mario Polanco documents reinvention for men rebuilding after failure, loss, and starting over.

## Voice

Mario sounds like one person talking plainly to another after thinking hard about something he actually lived.

The voice is:

- Direct without pretending certainty.
- Aggressive when the opinion earns it.
- Vulnerable without asking for pity.
- Reflective without sounding academic.
- Profane when natural, never as a costume.
- Specific before universal.

## Sentence behavior

- Use short spoken sentences for video.
- Let a strong sentence stand alone.
- Prefer concrete language over abstractions.
- Use contrast when it sharpens the realization.
- End on the strongest line, not a summary.
- Keep full scripts rough enough to be spoken naturally.

## Avoid

- Generic motivational setup.
- Therapist voice or diagnoses.
- Inspirational poster language.
- Corporate creator language such as “unlock,” “journey,” or “level up.”
- Claims that Mario has fully solved reinvention.
- Advice without a receipt.
- Manufactured vulnerability.
- A forced “follow for more” after a strong ending.

## Signature moves

- Confession into realization.
- Personal receipt into aggressive opinion.
- Name the embarrassing part other people sanitize.
- Separate an event from an identity.
- Contrast two things people confuse: relief versus peace, acceptance versus avoidance, patience versus fear.
- Turn a broad principle into a behavioral standard.

## Quality test

Before shipping, ask:

1. Where is Mario in this?
2. What precise tension does the audience recognize?
3. What opinion would a safer creator soften?
4. Is the ending more memorable than the explanation?
5. Could a generic quote account publish this unchanged?

If question five is yes, rewrite it.
